# ACGME Internal Medicine Milestones — Supplemental Guide

Source: Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME)
Date: November 2020

## About This Guide

This document provides additional guidance and examples for the Internal Medicine Milestones. It is not designed to indicate specific requirements for each level, but to provide insight into the thinking of the Milestone Work Group.

For each sub-competency it includes: the overall intent of the milestone, examples of what a Clinical Competency Committee (CCC) might expect to be observed or assessed at each level, and suggested assessment models and tools. Programs are encouraged to review this guide with the CCC and faculty members and to develop a shared mental model of the Milestones, optionally creating an individualized guide with institution- and program-specific examples.

Note on the examples below: they illustrate the intent of each level. They are not required elements or outcomes.

## Patient Care

### Patient Care 1: History
*Competency Domain: Patient Care*

**Overall Intent:** To competently interact with patients from heterogeneous backgrounds and consistently use all available resources to obtain a comprehensive patient history.

**Level 1** — Elicits and reports a comprehensive history for common patient presentations, with guidance; seeks data from secondary sources, with guidance.
- Obtains an accurate, patient-centered history from a 30-year-old with a red swollen joint using open-ended and directed questions, but without exploring clear underlying hypotheses.
- Presents an oral and written report that is organized but not focused on the chief complaint.
- Needs prompting to seek data from family members, ancillary staff, outside pharmacy, outside labs, and controlled-substance databases.

**Level 2** — Elicits and concisely reports a hypothesis-driven patient history for common presentations; independently obtains data from secondary sources.
- Interviews a patient with a red swollen joint, asking about recent alcohol use, diet, trauma, and sexual history; reports a history limited to pertinent positives and negatives.
- Without prompting, reviews and presents relevant data from prior records, labs, primary care notes, family, pharmacy, and controlled-substance databases.
- Proactively reviews prescription history and calls the pharmacy, noting that allopurinol has not been refilled in months.

**Level 3** — Elicits and concisely reports a hypothesis-driven history for complex presentations; reconciles current data with secondary sources.
- Presents an 85-year-old with heart failure, CAD, COPD, and diabetes complaining of weeks of dyspnea, asking about medication and dietary adherence, angina or heart failure symptoms, recent infection, and allergen exposure.
- Completes accurate medication reconciliation using multiple sources and clarifies the history as new information emerges from caregivers (e.g., recent weight gain).

**Level 4** — Efficiently elicits and concisely reports a history incorporating pertinent psychosocial and other determinants of health; uses history and secondary data to guide further diagnostic testing.
- Discovers the patient has not filled prescriptions due to an insurance lapse and unreliable transportation to a pharmacy.
- Determines the patient recently had a cardiac work-up at another hospital and does not reorder an echocardiogram based on prior results.

**Level 5** — Efficiently and effectively tailors history taking, including relevant subtleties, based on patient, family, and system needs; models effective use of history to guide further testing.
- Builds trust to explore relevant history in a patient with macrocytosis, gout, and abnormal liver tests, learning the patient consumes alcohol despite initial denial.
- Recognizes that a boyfriend answering all questions and non-verbal cues may indicate intimate partner violence.
- Illustrates to junior learners which history elements preclude the need for additional testing in a patient with headache.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Chart-stimulated recall; direct observation; medical record (chart) audit; OSCE; simulation; mini-CEX.

### Patient Care 2: Physical Examination
*Competency Domain: Patient Care*

**Overall Intent:** To perform a respectful and complete physical exam appropriate to the context of the visit.

**Level 1** — Performs a general physical examination while attending to patient comfort and safety; identifies common abnormal findings.
- Performs a comprehensive exam on a bedbound elderly patient, rolling the patient to assess the back while minimizing discomfort.
- Identifies a holosystolic murmur in a 35-year-old.

**Level 2** — Performs a hypothesis-driven exam for a common presentation; interprets common abnormal findings.
- Keeps an exam focused on local musculoskeletal, vascular, and neurologic systems in a 25-year-old with ankle pain after running.
- Identifies a systolic murmur radiating to the carotids and prioritizes aortic stenosis.
- Recognizes scleral icterus and orders liver function tests.

**Level 3** — Performs a hypothesis-driven exam for a complex presentation; identifies and interprets uncommon and complex abnormal findings.
- In an 85-year-old with multiple comorbidities and dyspnea, evaluates for JVD, hepatojugular reflux, lower-extremity edema, wheezing, pallor, and new murmurs.
- Identifies a diastolic murmur with splinter hemorrhages and recognizes potential endocarditis.

**Level 4** — Uses advanced maneuvers to elicit subtle findings; integrates subtle findings to guide diagnosis and management.
- Performs Valsalva, leg-raising, and squatting maneuvers to distinguish the cause of a new heart murmur.
- Identifies a pulsatile abdominal mass, prompting urgent imaging.
- Recognizes a concerning pigmented lesion on the heel of a patient with dark skin and refers for possible acral lentiginous melanoma.

**Level 5** — Models effective evidence-based exam technique; teaches the predictive values of findings to guide diagnosis and management.
- Demonstrates evidence-based techniques for evaluating rotator cuff impingement and articulates their diagnostic significance.
- Teaches that the presence of JVD rules in volume overload more effectively than its absence rules it out.
- Uses the Ottawa ankle criteria to forgo imaging in a patient with ankle pain after a fall.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Chart-stimulated recall; direct observation; medical record (chart) audit; OSCE; simulation.

### Patient Care 3: Clinical Reasoning
*Competency Domain: Patient Care*

**Overall Intent:** To consistently develop a complete and prioritized differential diagnosis while minimizing the impact of cognitive errors.

**Level 1** — Organizes and accurately summarizes information from the patient evaluation to develop a clinical impression.
- States that a 27-year-old woman on oral contraceptives presents with acute pleuritic right-sided chest pain and dyspnea after a 12-hour car trip.

**Level 2** — Integrates information from all sources to develop a basic differential for common presentations; identifies clinical reasoning errors, with guidance.
- Develops a differential of appendicitis, ectopic pregnancy, and ovarian torsion for a 25-year-old with acute right-lower-quadrant pain.
- With a senior physician, identifies premature closure as the reason lupus was excluded in a young woman on oral contraceptives with a malar rash.
- Recognizes bias as a reason thyroid disease was not considered in a Hispanic woman with weight gain and fatigue.

**Level 3** — Develops a thorough and prioritized differential for common presentations; retrospectively applies clinical reasoning principles to identify errors.
- For a 65-year-old with fatigue, weight loss, and low mood, posits major depression as most likely while considering apathetic hyperthyroidism, occult cancer, malabsorption, medication effects, or adrenal insufficiency.
- Recognizes the team anchored on benign positional vertigo when the diagnosis was a posterior circulation stroke.
- Reframes "non-compliance" by asking about food access and safe areas for exercise.
- Recognizes underdiagnosis of acute coronary syndromes in Black patients and advocates for evidence-based testing.

**Level 4** — Develops prioritized differentials in complex presentations, incorporating subtle, unusual, or conflicting findings; continually re-appraises one's own reasoning in real time.
- For an elderly patient with recurrent falls and subtle gait abnormality, considers normal pressure hydrocephalus, multisystem atrophy, and inclusion body myositis alongside common causes.
- Considers adrenal insufficiency when a patient with shock is not responding to appropriate antibiotics.

**Level 5** — Coaches others to develop prioritized differentials in complex presentations; models recognizing errors and reflecting on one's own reasoning.
- Teaches an intern to link missed associations to refine the differential in an unconscious ICU patient with multi-organ failure.
- Articulates how a posterior circulation stroke was missed due to anchoring and discusses how to change future transitions of care.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Chart-stimulated recall; direct observation; medical record (chart) audit; multisource feedback; reflection; simulation; evaluation of formal case presentations with explicit discussion of clinical reasoning (case conferences, M&M, etc.).

### Patient Care 4: Patient Management – Inpatient
*Competency Domain: Patient Care*

**Overall Intent:** To implement and follow through on comprehensive management plans for hospitalized patients.

**Level 1** — Formulates management plans for common conditions, with guidance; identifies opportunities to maintain and promote health.
- Creates an appropriate plan for community-acquired pneumonia after guidance from a senior physician.
- Identifies the need for tobacco cessation counseling and vaccinations.

**Level 2** — Develops and implements plans for common conditions, recognizing acuity and modifying based on clinical course; develops health-maintenance plans, with guidance.
- Correctly triages a COPD exacerbation needing a higher level of monitoring.
- Orders a renal ultrasound in a patient still febrile with flank pain 48 hours after UTI antibiotics.
- Anticipates and manages steroid-induced hyperglycemia in a patient with diabetes.
- Counsels a woman with alcoholic hepatitis about cessation and seeks guidance to refer her to treatment.

**Level 3** — Develops and implements value-based plans for multisystem/comorbid disease, modifying based on clinical course; independently develops health-maintenance plans incorporating psychosocial and other determinants of health.
- Orders antibiogram-appropriate broad-spectrum coverage for a patient with febrile neutropenia and new hypotension.
- Selects medications minimizing tachycardia and avoiding anticoagulant interactions in a patient with multiple comorbidities.
- Reviews low-cost medication options for an underinsured patient before discharge and ensures appropriate vaccinations after consent.
- Consults social work or chaplaincy for the sole survivor of a motor vehicle crash.

**Level 4** — Uses shared decision making to develop comprehensive value-based plans for comorbid/multisystem disease, including critical care; independently develops comprehensive health-maintenance plans incorporating psychosocial determinants.
- Uses evidence-based tools for peri-operative risk assessment in an elderly patient with hip fracture, diabetes, and CAD, avoiding blood transfusions for religious reasons.
- Weighs consultant recommendations for a 67-year-old with structural heart disease and syncope, ordering an echocardiogram but appropriately not a carotid ultrasound.
- Incorporates religious and cultural beliefs, adjusting insulin dosing during fasting.

**Level 5** — Develops and implements comprehensive plans for patients with rare or ambiguous presentations or unusual comorbidities.
- Selectively orders testing for pheochromocytoma or Wilson disease only with high pretest probability.
- Pursues a tiered, evidence-based approach to fatigue, fever of unknown origin, or failure to thrive in a developmentally disabled adult.
- Manages hypertensive crisis during pregnancy in a woman with lupus.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Case-based discussion; chart-stimulated recall; direct observation; medical record (chart) audit; multisource feedback; OSCE; reflective exercise; simulation.

### Patient Care 5: Patient Management – Outpatient
*Competency Domain: Patient Care*

**Overall Intent:** To implement and follow through on comprehensive management plans for outpatients.

**Level 1** — Identifies opportunities to maintain and promote health; formulates plans for a common chronic condition and for acute common conditions, with guidance.
- Notes a patient seen for an ankle sprain is due for pneumococcal vaccination.
- Formulates a plan for uncomplicated essential hypertension after discussion with a supervisor.
- Formulates a plan for an upper respiratory infection after discussion with a supervisor.

**Level 2** — Develops and implements health-maintenance plans, plans for common chronic conditions, and plans for common acute conditions.
- Discusses weight-bearing exercise and dietary calcium for bone health using EHR pre-created phrases.
- Advises regular, proper inhaled corticosteroid use for moderate persistent asthma.
- Implements an evidence-based plan for acute low back pain.

**Level 3** — Develops health-maintenance plans incorporating psychosocial determinants; plans for multiple chronic conditions; initial plans for urgent/emergent conditions in the setting of chronic comorbidities.
- Uses motivational interviewing for smoking cessation and refers a patient on a fixed income to a state quit-smoking program.
- Recognizes the patient lives in a food desert and connects them to a local food pantry.
- Adjusts medications to optimize glycemic and blood-pressure control in a patient with diabetes, stage IV CKD, and COPD.
- Initiates emergent care for anaphylaxis after a parenteral antibiotic in the office.

**Level 4** — Develops value-based comprehensive health-maintenance plans; value-based comprehensive plans for multiple chronic conditions incorporating psychosocial determinants; value-based plans for acute conditions.
- Overrides an EHR alert for a mammogram in a 60-year-old with metastatic lung cancer.
- Minimizes polypharmacy in an elderly patient with heart failure, kidney disease, COPD, cognitive impairment, and incontinence.
- Provides a home-based exercise plan for chronic low back pain when the patient cannot take time off work for physical therapy.
- Discusses acute gout therapeutic options including cost and availability through the patient's prescription plan.

**Level 5** — Creates and leads comprehensive patient-centered plans for highly complex chronic conditions, integrating multiple disciplines; develops plans for subtle, rare, or ambiguous presentations.
- Uses the EHR to coordinate cardiology, pain, orthopedics, and endocrinology for a complex patient with an LVAD, poorly controlled diabetes, and severe osteoarthritis.
- Develops a coordinated plan for a patient with frequent ED visits, poorly controlled asthma, bipolar disorder, substance use disorder, and unstable housing.
- Negotiates a referral to an undiagnosed-disease network to pursue mastocytosis after a negative initial work-up.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Chart-stimulated recall; direct observation; medical record (chart) audit; multisource feedback; OSCE; simulation.

**Note on high-value care:** The American College of Physicians defines high-value care as health care that balances clinical benefit with costs and harms, with the goal of improving patient outcomes. The Institute of Medicine defines it as the best care for the patient, with the optimal result for the circumstances, delivered at the right price.

### Patient Care 6: Digital Health
*Competency Domain: Patient Care*

**Overall Intent:** To optimally use EHRs and technology to enhance patient care, reflecting the increasing role of technology in patient management.

**Level 1** — Uses the EHR for routine patient care; identifies the required components for a telehealth visit.
- Manages the clinical inbox, enters basic orders, documents encounters, and reviews clinical data (labs, radiology, medication lists, other notes).
- Identifies secure telehealth software and patient access to a smartphone or computer with a microphone as requirements.

**Level 2** — Expands EHR use to include and reconcile secondary data sources; performs assigned telehealth visits using approved technology.
- Performs accurate medication reconciliation, updating the EHR on admission with outside pharmacy and hospital data.
- Initiates an e-consult and communicates through approved electronic systems (patient portal, secure email).

**Level 3** — Effectively uses EHR capabilities in acute and chronic care; identifies clinical situations manageable through telehealth.
- Uses health-maintenance tools, smart phrases, order sets, and templates; communicates with the team through the EHR.
- On a phone call, recognizes the need to assess a patient in person.
- Uses electronic data (blood pressure, glucose downloads, INR monitoring) to support management.

**Level 4** — Uses the EHR to achieve quality targets for patient panels; integrates telehealth effectively into acute and chronic care.
- Achieves a panel's pap-smear completion target through ongoing review.
- Reschedules an in-person visit to telehealth (or vice versa) based on clinical need.

**Level 5** — Leads improvements to the EHR; develops and innovates new uses of emerging technologies to augment telehealth.
- Serves as an EHR super-user, develops clinical decision pathways, and serves on EHR steering committees.
- Manages a patient from diagnosis through treatment digitally, including data review, consultant referral, and treatment initiation.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Medical record (chart) audit; chart-stimulated recall; simulation; direct observation; multisource feedback; portfolio; telehealth patient log; quality dashboard.

## Medical Knowledge

### Medical Knowledge 1: Applied Foundational Sciences
*Competency Domain: Medical Knowledge*

**Overall Intent:** To ensure attainment of medical knowledge that underlies the care of patients across the spectrum of health and disease.

**Level 1** — Explains the scientific knowledge (physiology, social sciences, mechanism of disease) for normal function and common conditions.
- Explains physiologic changes with aging; the anatomical basis of carpal tunnel syndrome; the epidemiology of hepatitis C screening; the microbiology of cellulitis; the role of socioeconomic status in asthma; ACE-inhibitor cough; and the impact of social determinants of health.

**Level 2** — Explains the scientific knowledge for complex conditions.
- Explains the physiology of multi-organ failure in sepsis; the biochemistry of diabetic ketoacidosis; nutritional deficiencies after bariatric surgery; and the pathophysiology of elevated parathyroid hormone.

**Level 3** — Integrates scientific knowledge to address comorbid conditions within multisystem disease.
- Integrates the pathophysiology of bone disease in CKD; the physiology of a heart-failure exacerbation; how diabetes affects vascular and renal systems in peripheral artery disease; and social determinants in diabetes management.

**Level 4** — Integrates scientific knowledge to address uncommon, atypical, or complex comorbid conditions within multisystem disease.
- Determines risk-based screening for adult survivors of childhood cancers.
- Applies pathophysiology of hepatorenal syndrome to manage a patient appropriately.
- Recognizes when a severity screening tool uses a proxy for social determinants and adjusts recommendations accordingly.

**Level 5** — Demonstrates a nuanced understanding of scientific knowledge related to uncommon, atypical, or complex conditions.
- Analyzes evolving evidence on the pathophysiology of postural orthostatic hypotensive syndrome in a patient with pre-existing hypertension.
- Investigates evolving evidence on the impact of racism on health to question previously held biases, policies, and procedures.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Chart-stimulated recall; direct observation (bedside, meetings, conferences); end-of-rotation evaluation; in-training examination; online question banks.

**Note:** "Common" clinical conditions vary by program and site but should include diseases most frequently encountered in inpatient and outpatient settings. "Complex" conditions are those less frequently encountered and those involving emerging or conflicting scientific knowledge.

### Medical Knowledge 2: Therapeutic Knowledge
*Competency Domain: Medical Knowledge*

**Overall Intent:** To ensure attainment of medical knowledge that guides therapeutic interventions across the spectrum of disease.

**Level 1** — Explains the scientific basis for common therapies.
- Describes the mechanism of action of ACE inhibitors in hypertension and of penicillin.

**Level 2** — Explains the indications, contraindications, risks, and benefits of common therapies.
- Describes the risks and benefits of therapeutic thoracentesis for a parapneumonic effusion and the indications/complications of diuretics in heart failure.
- Uses prior culture data showing an ESBL organism to narrow antibiotic coverage for stewardship.

**Level 3** — Integrates knowledge of therapeutic options in patients with comorbid conditions, multisystem disease, or uncertain diagnosis.
- Uses evidence-based heart-failure treatment in the context of diabetes and renal failure.
- Determines antimicrobial therapy for community-acquired pneumonia in a patient with alcohol withdrawal and QTc prolongation.
- Considers the renal risk of therapeutic paracentesis in decompensated cirrhosis.

**Level 4** — Integrates therapeutic options within the clinical and psychosocial context of the patient to formulate treatment options.
- Incorporates the risks of insulin therapy for a patient with diabetes who is homeless.
- Determines optimal chronic pain management for a patient with CKD, diabetes, chronic liver disease, and depression.
- Advocates for coronary artery bypass surgery for a Black woman with multi-vessel disease after discussing priorities and reviewing literature on barriers to care.

**Level 5** — Demonstrates a nuanced understanding of emerging, atypical, or complex therapeutic options.
- Describes the rationale for treating hyperlipidemia with an emerging monoclonal antibody (e.g., a PCSK9 inhibitor).
- Recognizes autoimmune thyroid complications of PD-1 checkpoint inhibitors during cancer treatment.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Direct observation (bedside, meetings, conferences); high-fidelity clinical decision simulation; in-training examination; online question banks; review of clinical experiences via the EHR.

**Note:** Therapies can include both pharmacologic and procedural modalities.

### Medical Knowledge 3: Knowledge of Diagnostic Testing
*Competency Domain: Medical Knowledge*

**Overall Intent:** To ensure attainment of medical knowledge that guides diagnostic testing across disease states.

**Level 1** — Explains the rationale, risks, and benefits of common diagnostic testing; interprets results of common tests.
- Explains the rationale for an EKG in chest pain and interprets flipped T waves.
- Explains the rationale and interprets a urinalysis in acute kidney injury.
- Explains the choice to image or forgo imaging in a knee injury.

**Level 2** — Explains the rationale, risks, and benefits of complex diagnostic testing; interprets complex diagnostic data.
- Explains the rationale, risks, and benefits of obtaining (or not) a D-dimer in dyspnea.
- Does not assume a positive antinuclear antibody equates to autoimmune disease.

**Level 3** — Integrates value and test characteristics of diagnostic strategies in patients with common diseases; integrates complex data to reach high-probability diagnoses.
- Compares the test characteristics of an exercise stress test versus a pharmacologic nuclear study in angina.
- Recognizes likely bias in creatinine-clearance measures and corrects for it.
- Interprets an arterial blood gas and metabolic panel to determine coexisting acid-base disorders.

**Level 4** — Integrates value and test characteristics in patients with comorbid conditions or multisystem disease; anticipates and accounts for limitations when interpreting data.
- Compares colon cancer screening methods in a patient on anticoagulation for a mechanical valve.
- Accounts for a low HbA1c in a patient with significantly elevated home glucose values.

**Level 5** — Demonstrates a nuanced understanding of emerging diagnostic tests and procedures.
- Discusses the application of a new genetic array in cancer staging.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Case-based discussions; chart documentation and review; direct observation; global assessment; in-training examination; online question banks; OSCE; portfolios.

## Systems-Based Practice

### Systems-Based Practice 1: Patient Safety and Quality Improvement
*Competency Domain: Systems-Based Practice*

**Overall Intent:** To develop knowledge, skill, and experience with patient safety and quality improvement.

**Level 1** — Demonstrates knowledge of common patient safety events, how to report them, and basic QI methodologies and metrics.
- Identifies a medication error caught by pharmacy as a near miss and distinguishes it from an adverse event.
- Describes institutional reporting pathways (incident reporting systems, confidential hotline).
- Describes QI frameworks (Model for Improvement, LEAN, Six Sigma) and the purpose of root cause analysis.

**Level 2** — Identifies system factors leading to safety events; reports events through institutional systems; describes local QI initiatives.
- Describes how high workload and communication failures contributed to an event.
- Notes an incorrect infection-control sign and files a patient safety event report in addition to contacting the bedside nurse and manager.
- Describes an influenza vaccination program as a local QI initiative.

**Level 3** — Contributes to the analysis of safety events; participates in disclosure to patients and families; contributes to local QI initiatives.
- Reviews a safety event for an M&M conference presentation.
- Participates as a team member when a senior resident discloses an iatrogenic pneumothorax.
- Collects data for a hypertension QI project addressing control disparities between white and Black patients.

**Level 4** — Conducts analysis of safety events and offers prevention strategies; discloses events to patients and families; demonstrates skills to identify, develop, implement, and analyze a QI project.
- Analyzes contributions to a patient fall and offers strategies to reduce sedating medication.
- Leads disclosure of an iatrogenic pneumothorax in a simulated experience.
- Designs and implements a QI project on zoster vaccination in patients over 50, making iterative changes.

**Level 5** — Leads teams and processes to modify systems to prevent safety events; models disclosure; creates, implements, and assesses sustainable QI initiatives at the institutional or community level.
- Assumes a leadership role for a safety initiative improving all ICU-to-floor transfers.
- Role-plays disclosure with a first-year resident who will participate in disclosing a team error.
- Works with the EHR team to add age-appropriate hepatitis C screening after a successful pilot.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Chart or system documentation; conference presentation with evaluation; direct observation; multisource feedback; portfolio; QI Knowledge Assessment Tool; simulation; SQUIRE guidelines; poster or abstract QI presentation with feedback.

### Systems-Based Practice 2: System Navigation for Patient-Centered Care
*Competency Domain: Systems-Based Practice*

**Overall Intent:** To effectively navigate the health care system, including the interdisciplinary team and other providers, and to adapt care to a specific patient population to ensure high-quality outcomes.

**Level 1** — Demonstrates knowledge of care coordination, key elements of safe transitions/hand-offs, and population/community health needs.
- Identifies social workers and case managers as part of the care team.
- Identifies code status, allergies, and pending labs as key hand-off elements.
- Recognizes bias as a contributor to health care barriers.

**Level 2** — Coordinates care via interprofessional teams in routine situations; performs safe transitions in routine situations; identifies local population/community health needs.
- Engages the case manager to arrange home oxygen for a COPD discharge; consults chaplaincy for a patient fearing death.
- Performs medication reconciliation and accurate documentation when discharging to a long-term care facility.
- Identifies a Nepali refugee population at high risk for thyroid disease due to iodine deficiency; identifies food deserts contributing to local obesity.

**Level 3** — Coordinates care via interprofessional teams in complex situations; performs safe transitions in complex situations; uses local resources effectively to meet population and community needs.
- For a patient with new lung cancer and severe malnutrition, works with nutrition, respiratory therapy, and physical therapy.
- Uses a structured format for a comprehensive ICU hand-off.
- Provides information on a local food bank and dental clinic; enlists a community health worker to support a struggling patient.

**Level 4** — Models effective coordination across disciplines; models and advocates for safe transitions within and across delivery systems, including outpatient; participates in adapting practice for specific populations.
- Includes case manager, social worker, pharmacist, and diabetes educator in rounds to arrange safe discharge for a patient with a diabetic foot ulcer, osteomyelitis, and homelessness.
- Helps implement a literacy screening tool in clinic to identify populations needing alternative education materials.

**Level 5** — Analyzes the care-coordination process and leads design and implementation of improvements; improves quality of transitions to optimize outcomes; leads innovations and advocates for populations and communities.
- Works with the clinic nurse manager to restructure appointments to reduce no-shows and improve access.
- Modifies late-appointment policies that disproportionately impact patients reliant on public transportation.
- Designs a home-visit program to improve vaccination rates in an elderly refugee population.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Direct observation; medical record (chart) audit; multisource feedback; OSCE; portfolio; review of sign-out tools; simulation.

### Systems-Based Practice 3: Physician Role in Health Care Systems
*Competency Domain: Systems-Based Practice*

**Overall Intent:** To understand the physician's role in the complex health care system and how to optimize the system to improve patient care and system performance.

**Level 1** — Identifies key components of the health care system; describes basic health payment systems.
- Differentiates skilled nursing facilities from nursing homes.
- Identifies hospital finance, technology, and support services as essential components.
- Describes Medicare, Medicaid, VA, and commercial third-party payers.

**Level 2** — Describes how complex-system components are interrelated and how this impacts care; delivers care considering each patient's payment model.
- Acknowledges that timely discharge maintains bed availability and community ED access.
- Checks insurance status before prescribing a higher-priced antihypertensive.

**Level 3** — Discusses how individual practice affects the regional and national system; engages patients in shared decision making informed by payment models.
- Discusses how timeliness in follow-up of results affects length of stay and how documentation impacts outcome measures and financial status.
- Discusses anticoagulation choice including cost, convenience, and safety.
- Leads a discussion with a patient whose high copay makes him ambivalent about a sleep study.

**Level 4** — Manages components of the complex system to provide efficient, effective care; advocates for patient needs considering payment-model limitations.
- Works with the discharge team to complete home hospice enrollment.
- Uses CMS criteria to assign observation versus inpatient status.
- Identifies a consulting practice accessible by public transportation for patients with limited transportation.

**Level 5** — Advocates for or leads systems change that enhances high-value, efficient, effective care; actively engaged in influencing health policy through advocacy at local, regional, or national levels.
- Works with the community health department to enable resident participation in a mobile clinic.
- Improves the institutional informed-consent process for non-English-speaking patients requiring interpreters.
- Works with physician society groups to advocate for lower insulin pricing.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** CV review; direct observation; medical record (chart) audit; multisource feedback; portfolio; procedure log; QI project.

## Practice-Based Learning and Improvement

### Practice-Based Learning and Improvement 1: Evidence-Based and Informed Practice
*Competency Domain: Practice-Based Learning and Improvement*

**Overall Intent:** To incorporate evidence and patient values into clinical practice.

**Level 1** — Demonstrates how to access, categorize, and analyze clinical evidence, with guidance.
- Identifies evidence-based guidelines for osteoporosis screening at the USPSTF website; actively participates in journal club.

**Level 2** — Articulates clinical questions and elicits patient preferences and values to guide evidence-based care.
- For a patient with hyperlipidemia, identifies and discusses evidence-based treatment options and solicits the patient's perspective.

**Level 3** — Critically appraises and applies the best available evidence, integrated with patient preference, to the care of complex patients.
- Obtains, discusses, and applies evidence for a patient with hyperlipidemia, diabetes, and hypertension.
- Appropriately uses clinical practice guidelines while eliciting patient preferences.

**Level 4** — Applies evidence, even amid uncertainty and conflicting evidence, to guide care tailored to the individual patient.
- Accesses primary literature to identify alternatives to bisphosphonates for osteoporosis.

**Level 5** — Coaches others to critically appraise and apply evidence to patient care.
- Leads clinical teaching on critical appraisal of sepsis criteria.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Chart-stimulated recall; direct observation; evaluation of a presentation; journal club and case-based discussion; multisource feedback; oral or written examination; portfolio; simulation.

### Practice-Based Learning and Improvement 2: Reflective Practice and Commitment to Personal Growth
*Competency Domain: Practice-Based Learning and Improvement*

**Overall Intent:** To seek clinical performance information with the intent to improve care; reflect on all domains of practice, personal interactions, and behaviors and their impact on colleagues and patients (reflective mindfulness); and develop clear objectives and goals for improvement in the form of a learning plan.

**Level 1** — Accepts responsibility for development by establishing goals; identifies factors contributing to gaps between ideal and actual performance, with guidance.
- Acknowledges knowledge deficits after an ICU rotation and sets a goal to review objectives before the next rotation.
- After a readmission, identifies incomplete medication reconciliation at discharge as a contributing factor.

**Level 2** — Demonstrates openness to performance data to inform goals; analyzes and reflects on factors contributing to gaps, with guidance; actively seeks opportunities to improve.
- Uses feedback from a difficult staff interaction to set a goal of closed-loop communication.
- After an alert about a missed clinic lab result, makes a goal to check the EHR inbox between clinics.
- Recognizes how biases may have impacted an interaction and calls to apologize after a complaint.

**Level 3** — Seeks performance data episodically, with adaptability and humility; institutes behavioral changes to narrow gaps; designs and implements an individualized learning plan, with prompting.
- Requests feedback after an end-of-life discussion goes poorly.
- Prepares for clinic within the EHR to improve efficiency and note completion.
- With an advisor, designs a study plan to improve critical care knowledge.

**Level 4** — Seeks performance data consistently, with adaptability and humility; challenges one's own assumptions and considers alternatives in narrowing gaps; independently creates and implements an individualized learning plan.
- Requests weekly feedback on leadership skills from the attending after ward rounds.
- Recognizes prior bias and blind spots in managing chronic pain and seeks additional training.
- Identifies a research mentor and schedules chart review over the year.

**Level 5** — Models consistently seeking performance data with adaptability and humility; coaches others on reflective practice; uses performance data to measure and improve the effectiveness of the learning plan.
- Routinely initiates team discussion on readmissions and openly analyzes opportunities for improvement, including self.
- At the end of a ward rotation, asks junior learners what went well and designs plans to meet their goals.
- After recognizing that current efforts to improve communication have been ineffective, seeks further professional coaching.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Direct observation; multisource feedback; portfolio; reflection; review of learning plan; self-assessment.

## Professionalism

### Professionalism 1: Professional Behavior
*Competency Domain: Professionalism*

**Overall Intent:** To recognize and address lapses in ethical and professional behavior, demonstrate ethical and professional behaviors, and use appropriate resources for managing ethical and professional dilemmas.

**Level 1** — Demonstrates professional behavior in routine situations.
- Informs the program when arriving late on a call day; dresses appropriately; completes mandatory compliance training without reminders; responds to program emails promptly.

**Level 2** — Identifies potential triggers for professionalism lapses and accepts responsibility for one's own lapses.
- Receives feedback about being late without becoming defensive or blaming others.
- After using a sharp tone with a colleague, apologizes and recognizes the behavior is more common when excessively tired.
- Recognizes that personal life issues are affecting work interactions.

**Level 3** — Demonstrates a pattern of professional behavior in complex or stressful situations.
- Maintains a calm demeanor and even tone with a difficult patient during a night shift.
- Acknowledges responsibility and takes part in disclosure when involved with a medical error.

**Level 4** — Recognizes situations that may trigger lapses and intervenes to prevent lapses in oneself and others.
- Monitors and responds to fatigue, hunger, and stress in self and team members post-call.
- Notifies the program director when a colleague is noticeably struggling with workload or personal stress.

**Level 5** — Coaches others when their behavior fails to meet professional expectations.
- Coaches a first-year resident who was rude to a nurse to apologize.
- Pulls aside a habitually late colleague to explain accountability and impact on others.
- Leads a residency workshop on microaggressions and bias.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Direct observation; end-of-rotation evaluation; mentor and program director observations; multisource feedback; oral or written self-reflection; professionalism tools; semi-annual evaluation; simulation.

### Professionalism 2: Ethical Principles
*Competency Domain: Professionalism*

**Overall Intent:** To recognize and address lapses in ethical and professional behavior, demonstrate ethical and professional behaviors, and use appropriate resources for managing ethical and professional dilemmas.

**Level 1** — Demonstrates knowledge of basic ethical principles.
- Names the principles of autonomy and non-maleficence.

**Level 2** — Applies basic principles to address straightforward ethical situations.
- Discusses the ethical principles of performing a paracentesis in a patient with ascites, abdominal pain, and clear decision-making capacity.
- Discusses the pros and cons of feeding-tube placement in a patient with brain death.

**Level 3** — Analyzes complex situations using ethical principles and identifies the need to seek help.
- Analyzes a paracentesis in a patient with altered mental status and involves the durable power of attorney.
- Contacts an ethics consult in an end-of-life situation with complex family dynamics and mistrust of the system.

**Level 4** — Analyzes complex situations and engages appropriate resources for managing ethical dilemmas as needed.
- Works with the ethics committee and family for a patient suspected of malingering.
- Engages a multidisciplinary team when family and physicians disagree about a brain-death care plan, recognizing how prior experiences influence trust.

**Level 5** — Identifies and seeks to address system-level factors that induce or exacerbate ethical problems or impede their resolution.
- Participates in an ethics committee, risk management committee, root cause analysis review, IRB, or similar group.
- Adeptly manages ethical conflicts when family values conflict and there is no clear durable power of attorney.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Direct observation (clinical or classroom); mentor and program director observations; multisource feedback; oral or written self-reflection; simulation.

**Note:** Residents should discuss and apply the basic principles underlying ethics (beneficence, non-maleficence, justice, autonomy) and professionalism to clinical scenarios (authentic or simulated).

### Professionalism 3: Accountability/Conscientiousness
*Competency Domain: Professionalism*

**Overall Intent:** To take responsibility for one's own actions and their impact on patients and other members of the health care team.

**Level 1** — Performs administrative tasks and patient care responsibilities, with prompting.
- Responds to prompting to complete work-hour logs, clinic notes, discharge summaries, compliance training, and program emails.

**Level 2** — Performs administrative tasks and responsibilities in a timely manner in routine situations.
- Completes discharge summaries promptly with attention to detail without prompting.
- Completes compliance training and responds to emails without reminders; returns clinic patient calls by end of day.

**Level 3** — Performs administrative tasks and responsibilities in a timely manner in complex or stressful situations.
- Ensures safe hand-off after a busy shift with multiple decompensating patients.
- Identifies influenza in self and calls in a back-up resident to cover clinical services.

**Level 4** — Proactively implements strategies to ensure the needs of patients, teams, and systems are met.
- Helps a first-year resident who chronically runs behind in clinic develop a more efficient workflow.
- Recognizes an overwhelmed ICU team member and asks the attending for support.
- Develops time-management strategies for on-time completion of board applications and ACLS renewals.

**Level 5** — Creates strategies to enhance others' ability to efficiently complete administrative tasks and responsibilities.
- Works with the EHR team to develop an efficient hand-off tool.
- Develops strategies to help incoming interns learn the EHR from a physician perspective.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Compliance with deadlines and timelines; direct observation; documentation of mentor and program leadership observations; multisource feedback; self-evaluations and reflective tools; simulation.

### Professionalism 4: Knowledge of Systemic and Individual Factors of Well-Being
*Competency Domain: Professionalism*

**Overall Intent:** To identify, use, manage, improve, or seek help for personal and professional growth within self and others.

**Important note:** This sub-competency is not intended to evaluate a resident's well-being. The intent is to ensure each resident has fundamental knowledge of the factors that impact well-being, the mechanisms by which they do so, and the available resources and tools to improve well-being.

**Level 1** — Recognizes the importance of getting help when needed to address personal and professional well-being.
- After a program leader expresses concern about well-being or burnout, is receptive to options for assistance.
- Acknowledges a chief resident's or supervisor's outreach about possible burnout as a form of professional support.

**Level 2** — Lists resources to support well-being; recognizes that institutional factors affect well-being.
- Discusses institutional well-being resources in an annual advisor meeting.
- After learning modules, articulates how institutional factors may impact resident well-being.
- Identifies microaggressions or bias as factors affecting learner well-being.

**Level 3** — With prompting, reflects on how well-being may impact one's clinical practice; describes institutional factors that affect well-being.
- Writes a brief reflection on the impact of well-being on current and future practice after a retreat.
- At semiannual review, identifies specific institutional factors (e.g., lack of healthy food in the cafeteria, insufficient social work support) that affect personal well-being.
- Identifies the need for additional mentorship after discovering initial career plans do not align with personal goals.

**Level 4** — Reflects on actions in real time to proactively respond to the emotional challenges of physician work; suggests potential solutions to institutional factors that affect well-being.
- Prepares a robust board study schedule to minimize stress; arranges sim-lab practice sessions to address procedure-related anxiety.
- Proactively reaches out to program leadership for support while grieving a personal loss.
- Recommends schedule adjustments on the MICU rotation to improve compliance with work hours.

**Level 5** — Participates in institutional changes to promote personal and professional well-being.
- Develops a plan incorporating personal wellness goals and schedules restorative activities around an emotionally draining rotation.
- Explores new approaches (telecommunication, distanced socializing) to maintain peer emotional support during pandemic conditions.
- Advocates with hospital leadership as a Well-Being Committee leader for educational interventions and mental health services.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Direct observation; group interview or discussions; individual interview; institutional online training modules; reflective writing; self-assessment and personal learning plan; semi-annual evaluation.

## Interpersonal and Communication Skills

### Interpersonal and Communication Skills 1: Patient- and Family-Centered Communication
*Competency Domain: Interpersonal and Communication Skills*

**Overall Intent:** To deliberately use language and behaviors to form constructive relationships with patients; to identify communication barriers (including self-reflection on biases) and minimize them; and to organize and lead communication around shared decision making.

**Level 1** — Uses language and non-verbal behavior to demonstrate respect and establish rapport.
- Controls tone and non-verbal responses and asks questions to invite patient/family participation.
- Accurately communicates the resident's role to patients and families.

**Level 2** — Establishes and maintains a therapeutic relationship using effective communication in straightforward encounters; identifies common barriers to effective communication.
- Explains the rationale for not prescribing antibiotics for acute bronchitis while conveying empathy.
- Recognizes through teach-back that a patient does not understand a treatment plan; recognizes a patient cannot hear without a hearing aid.
- Avoids medical jargon and pitches communication to the patient's level.

**Level 3** — Establishes and maintains a therapeutic relationship in challenging encounters; identifies complex barriers to communication.
- Circles back with a patient frustrated about being NPO all day due to procedure delays.
- Discusses a non-opioid pain plan with a patient previously on chronic opioids.
- Recognizes that bias may impact communication with a patient with substance use disorder requiring pain medication.

**Level 4** — Establishes and maintains therapeutic relationships using shared decision making, regardless of complexity; mitigates communication barriers.
- Practices shared decision making with a patient with dementia and family to set an outpatient A1c target.
- Proactively arranges an interpreter at the bedside during rounds.
- Recognizes that mispronouncing a patient's name may be experienced as a microaggression, apologizes, and corrects it.

**Level 5** — Coaches others in developing and maintaining therapeutic relationships and mitigating barriers; models the mitigation of communication barriers.
- Guides a junior learner on re-establishing a therapeutic relationship after a breach.
- Shares how perspective-taking can reduce the impact of bias on patient care.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Communication checklist; direct observation; self-assessment including self-reflection exercises; standardized patients or structured case discussions.

### Interpersonal and Communication Skills 2: Interprofessional and Team Communication
*Competency Domain: Interpersonal and Communication Skills*

**Overall Intent:** To effectively communicate with the health care team, including consultants, in both straightforward and complex situations.

**Level 1** — Respectfully requests and responds to a consultation; uses verbal and non-verbal communication that values all members of the interprofessional team.
- Responds to calls/pages in a timely fashion and expresses thanks; uses team members' names and appropriate titles.
- Asks for clarification of role when needed; avoids speaking over or interrupting; uses "I" statements when expressing a differing view.

**Level 2** — Clearly and concisely requests and responds to a consultation; communicates information, including basic feedback, with all team members.
- When requesting a consult, clearly states the question and summarizes the patient story; as a consultant, summarizes recommendations and reasons.
- Uses language understood by all team members and avoids unfamiliar abbreviations.
- Provides feedback to a medical student on improving an oral presentation.

**Level 3** — Checks own and others' understanding of recommendations when providing or receiving consultation; facilitates team communication to reconcile conflict and provides difficult feedback.
- Repeats back consultant input and asks for verification to ensure agreement; as a consultant, asks the receiver to repeat back recommendations.
- Seeks to understand the reason behind a nursing request when faced with frustration.
- Provides specific examples to a team member of what to continue and what to improve.

**Level 4** — Coordinates recommendations from different consultants to optimize care; adapts communication style to fit team needs and maximizes the impact of feedback.
- Coordinates with multiple consulting teams to negotiate a shared treatment plan.
- Adjusts from a supportive to a more direct style for a first-year resident who has not responded to initial feedback.

**Level 5** — Facilitates conflict resolution among consultants when disagreement exists; models flexible communication strategies that facilitate excellence in interprofessional teamwork.
- Facilitates a multidisciplinary meeting among interventional radiology, pulmonology, and thoracic surgery to decide who should perform a lung biopsy.
- Leads a post-code meeting to improve communication while attending to the team's emotional responses.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Direct observation; end-of-month feedback; multisource assessment; simulation encounters.

### Interpersonal and Communication Skills 3: Communication within Health Care Systems
*Competency Domain: Interpersonal and Communication Skills*

**Overall Intent:** To effectively communicate using a variety of methods in the context of the organization.

**Level 1** — Accurately documents comprehensive and current information; communicates using formats specified by institutional policy to safeguard patient personal health information.
- Notes are accurate and organized but may include extraneous information; daily progress notes have updated imaging and care plans.
- Ensures sensitive information is communicated through appropriate channels (patient safety reports, cell phone/pager protocols).

**Level 2** — Documents the clinical encounter, including reasoning, through organized notes; selects direct and indirect forms of communication based on context, with assistance.
- Provides an organized explanation of the diagnosis and plan for community-acquired pneumonia.
- Avoids biased or stigmatizing language (e.g., "declines treatment" rather than "refuses"; "doesn't use marijuana" rather than "denies use").
- Needs reminding to call a patient about an elevated potassium and discusses how best to document the telephone encounter.

**Level 3** — Documents the encounter through concise and thorough notes; appropriately selects direct and indirect forms of communication based on context.
- Documents thought process while omitting extraneous information in a patient with acute dyspnea, chronic heart failure, and iron-deficiency anemia.
- Calls a patient about a newly identified breast mass and electronically arranges follow-up with clinic staff.

**Level 4** — Documents clearly, concisely, timely, and in an organized form, including anticipatory guidance; models effective written and verbal communication.
- Documents a concise rationale and future work-up plan to transition a patient to an incoming intern.
- Orders a stress test with a plan for catheterization if positive; documents an antibiotic plan should a neutropenic patient develop a fever.
- Demonstrates for an intern how to call an oncologist to expedite follow-up for a recent cancer diagnosis.

**Level 5** — Guides departmental or institutional communication policies and procedures.
- Leads a QI-committee task force to improve house-staff hand-offs.
- Improves methods for communicating system-wide call schedules.

**Assessment Models or Tools:** Chart review for documented communications; direct observation of sign-outs and consultation requests; end-of-month evaluation or global assessment; multisource feedback; simulation.
